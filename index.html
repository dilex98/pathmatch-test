Path Match: frozen test copies

Not the live site. Jobs frozen as of Nov 13, 2026; the page clock is fixed to Nov 14, 2026, so every run gives the same result.

  • Before — today's matching, unchanged.
    The baseline for every comparison.
  • After 1 — phase 1: reading the CV.
    Reads PDFs by position, reads columns and sidebars, spots a file holding several versions of a CV, and refuses to rank when the CV could not be read.
  • After 2 — phase 2: dates and career stage.
    Finds what each date belongs to by looking around it, counts apprenticeships and student jobs as student work, understands "1 month each", and puts someone who is studying now with no job running now back at student level.
  • After 3 — phase 3: cleaning the words.
    Stops one word from switching on a skill the CV never claimed (patent portfolio was read as Financial analysis), keeps the names of employers and schools out of the skills, stops a French word matching an English one that only looks the same, cuts a posting's job IDs, contact names and postal codes out of its keywords, and leaves hobbies, prizes and high-school lines out of the matching.
  • After 4 — phases 4 and 5: one card per job, and the new score.
    The score is now Skills 70 + Education 20 + Credentials 10, out of the parts a posting actually asks for. The job title and the career stage give no points: the stage decides which jobs are in the list at all, and the field and the goal written on the CV decide the order. The same job posted twice is one card. A mandatory licence the CV does not have is a red flag and the job leaves the top 10.
  • After 5 — what you bring: credentials, recency, and the licence question.
    A degree or a certificate named on the CV now brings the skills behind it: a certificate's own subjects count in full, a degree's at half. Work older than about eight years counts half. A posting that demands a licence the CV does not show is no longer dropped in silence: it stays, quotes the employer's own sentence, and asks whether you have it.
  • After 6 — matching by meaning: tested, and not shipped.
    The last phase asked whether a skill a posting names could be matched to a different wording on the CV. It was measured on the real gaps and it does not work yet: at a cut-off strict enough to be safe it adds less than one half-credit to a posting missing 25 skills, and the clearest mistakes score higher than the genuine catches. The matching is unchanged from After 5. What the measurement found instead is a vocabulary gap, so After 6 adds the weekly report that lists the phrases many postings ask for that the dictionary does not know, for a person to approve.
  • Inference baseline — the live site of Nov 20, 2026, frozen for the inference test.
    New jobs (Nov 18, 2026 refresh: 80,291 postings) and a new fixed date (Nov 19, 2026), so compare it only with the inference copies that come after it, not with Before to After 6. Code from Molu221/Path_Match_V1 at a25d2e4.
  • Inference, Part 2 — skills that usually go with yours.
    Learned in Python from 12,207 real postings: which skills employers ask for together. The site asks the person about the likely ones ("often asked with AutoCAD and Tendering, which are on your CV"); a skill counts only once ticked, marked "you confirmed". On postings it never saw, 31.5% of its guesses were right, 2.8 times a plain popular guess. Compare with the Inference baseline. See infer-after/NOTES.md.
  • I-24: Gaps that are not skills — single words like "data" or "tracking", and degrees, no longer count as missing skills; the occupation reading lists skills only.
    About 30% fewer Gaps per posting for the four test CVs, with no score inflation. Built on the Inference baseline; compare with it. See gaps-after/NOTES.md.
